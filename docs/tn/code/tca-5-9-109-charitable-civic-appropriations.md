## Practitioner Summary
This section outlines the appropriation and disbursement of funds for charitable and civic organizations in Tennessee counties, providing guidelines on how to use the money for economic development, social welfare, and common good.

## Key Practice Points
* **Purpose**: The county legislative body or governing body may appropriate funds for nonprofit charitable and civic organizations.
* **Guidelines**: Appropriations must be used to promote the general welfare of residents in the county. Funds can be spent on annual reports detailing business affairs, program descriptions, and proposed uses.

## Statutory Text

The county legislative body or governing body of each county may appropriate funds for the financial aid of any nonprofit charitable organization, any chamber of commerce, exempt from taxation pursuant to the Internal Revenue Code of 1954, § 501(c)(6) (26 U.S.C. § 501(c)(6)), or any nonprofit civic organization in accordance with the guidelines required by subsection (b).

Each county legislative body shall devise guidelines directing for what purposes the appropriated money may be spent. These guidelines shall provide generally that any funds appropriated shall be used to promote the general welfare of the residents of the county. Any funds appropriated under this section shall be used and expended under the direction and control of the county legislative bodies.

Any nonprofit organization that desires financial assistance from the county legislative body or the governing body of the county shall file with the county clerk a copy of an annual report of its business affairs and transactions, which includes, but is not limited to, a copy of an annual audit, a description of the program that serves the residents of the county, and the proposed use of the county assistance.

Such report will be open for public inspection during the regular business hours of the county clerk's office. Any nonprofit organization that desires such financial assistance may file, in lieu of the annual audit, an annual report detailing all receipts and expenditures in a form prescribed by the comptroller of the treasury.

Financial reports shall be available to fiscal officers of the county and shall be subject to audit under § 4-3-304.
