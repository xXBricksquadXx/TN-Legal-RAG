## Practitioner Summary
This statute establishes the procedural framework for suing a county for the wrongful acts or omissions of a sheriff's deputy. It acts as a companion to the Governmental Tort Liability Act, specifically routing deputy liability to the county itself, provided the deputy was acting under the color of office.

## Key Practice Points
* **County Liability for Deputies**: Anyone suffering injury or damage from a deputy's actions may sue the county, rather than the sheriff personally, provided the deputy was acting by virtue of or under color of their office.
* **Strict Statute of Limitations**: Actions brought under this section must be filed within one (1) year after the cause of action accrues.
* **Extended Answer Period**: Unlike standard civil rules, the county is granted a sixty (60) day period to answer the complaint. Failure to answer within 60 days does not result in default; rather, it is deemed a denial of the complaint's material allegations.

## Statutory Text
(a) Anyone incurring any wrong, injury, loss, damage or expense resulting from any act or failure to act on the part of any deputy appointed by the sheriff may bring suit against the county in which the sheriff serves; provided, that the deputy is, at the time of such occurrence, acting by virtue of or under color of the office.
(b) Notwithstanding § 28-1-105, an action brought pursuant to subsection (a) must be filed within one (1) year after the cause of action accrued.
(c)(1) The county has sixty (60) days in which to answer or otherwise respond to any action brought pursuant to subsection (a).
(2) If, at the end of the sixty-day period, the county has failed to answer or otherwise respond to the complaint, the county shall not be in default but rather is deemed to have denied the material, well pleaded factual allegations of the complaint.
