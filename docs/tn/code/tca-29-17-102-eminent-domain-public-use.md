## Practitioner Summary
This statute strictly limits a governmental entity's power of eminent domain by explicitly defining what constitutes a "public use." It was enacted to prevent governments from seizing private property merely to transfer it to another private entity for economic development.

## Key Practice Points
* **Exclusions from Public Use**: Condemnation cannot be used for recreational facilities/parks, private use, or indirect public benefits resulting from private economic development.
* **Valid Public Uses**: Eminent domain is authorized for roads, bridges, public transportation, utility infrastructure, and the eradication of formally defined "blighted property."
* **Incidental Private Use**: Private use of condemned land is only allowed if it is purely incidental to the primary public use.

## Statutory Text
(1) “Eminent domain” means the authority conferred upon the government, and those entities to whom the government delegates such authority, to condemn and take, in whole or in part, the private property of another, so long as the property is taken for a legitimate public use...
(2) “Public use” does not include recreational facilities, recreational purposes, or parks; private use or benefit; or the indirect public benefits resulting from private economic development and private commercial enterprise, except as follows:
(A) The acquisition of any interest in land necessary for a road, highway, bridge, or other structure, facility, or project used for public transportation;
(B) The acquisition of any interest in land necessary to the function of a public or private utility...
(C) The acquisition of property by a housing authority or community development agency to implement an urban renewal or redevelopment plan... provided, that the power of eminent domain is used only for the acquisition of: (i) Blighted property...
