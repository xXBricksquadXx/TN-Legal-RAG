## Practitioner Summary
This statute defines the critical terms of the Open Meetings Act, specifically detailing what constitutes a "governing body" and a "meeting." It closes loopholes by confirming that "deliberating" toward a decision triggers the Act, and explicitly bans the use of electronic communications or "chance meetings" to circumvent the law.

## Key Practice Points
* **Triggering a "Meeting"**: A meeting occurs when a governing body convenes not just to make a decision, but to *deliberate* toward a decision.
* **Non-Profit Inclusion**: Certain private non-profit corporations become subject to TOMA if they contract with state agencies and receive at least 30% of their income from public funds.
* **The "Chance Meeting" Loophole**: While genuine chance meetings aren't violations, officials are strictly prohibited from using informal assemblages or electronic communication to decide or deliberate public business in secret.

## Statutory Text
(a) All meetings of any governing body are declared to be public meetings open to the public at all times, except as provided by the Constitution of Tennessee. 
(b)(1) "Governing body" means: (A) The members of a public body which consists of two (2) or more members, excluding a county mayor... with the authority to make decisions for or recommendations to a public body on policy or administration... (B) The board of directors of any nonprofit corporation which contracts with a state agency to receive community grant funds... provided, that community grant funds comprise at least thirty percent (30%) of the total annual income...
(2) "Meeting" means the convening of a governing body of a public body to make a decision or to deliberate toward a decision on any matter. "Meeting" does not include any on-site inspection of any project or program. 
(c) Nothing in this section shall be construed as to require a chance meeting of two (2) or more members of a public body to be considered a public meeting. No such chance meetings, informal assemblages, or electronic communication shall be used to decide or deliberate public business in circumvention of the spirit or requirements of this part.
