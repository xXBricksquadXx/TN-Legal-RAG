## Practitioner Summary
Part of the "Financial Management System of 1981," this section creates a centralized finance department responsible for purchasing, accounting, and budgeting for most county funds. It specifically protects the personnel and salary autonomy of constitutional officers.

## Key Practice Points
* **Centralization**: Transfers purchasing, payroll, and budgeting functions from individual operating departments to the Director of Finance.
* **Constitutional Officers**: Explicitly denies authority over the personnel policies or fee accounts of court clerks, the county clerk, register of deeds, sheriff, or trustee.
* **Accounting Standards**: Requires the fiscal system to conform to generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) and the state's uniform audit manual.
* **Exclusions**: The legislative body can vote by a two-thirds majority to exclude county hospitals or nursing homes from these centralized procedures.

## Statutory Text
(a)(1) There is hereby created a finance department to administer the finances of the county... (b) The finance department shall be responsible for purchasing, accounting, budgeting, payroll, cash management... (d)(2) The system shall conform to generally accepted accounting principles... (e)(1) ...the county legislative body may exclude, by two thirds (2/3) majority vote, the county hospitals or nursing homes... from this chapter.
