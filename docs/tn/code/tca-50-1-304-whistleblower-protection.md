## Practitioner Summary
Known as the Tennessee Public Employee Whistleblower Protection Act, this statute protects employees from retaliatory termination for refusing to participate in, or refusing to remain silent about, illegal activities. It abrogates common-law retaliatory discharge claims and sets a McDonnell Douglas burden-shifting framework for litigation.

## Key Practice Points
* **Protection Against Retaliation**: Employees cannot be terminated solely for refusing to engage in, or for exposing, illegal activities conducted by the employer or officials.
* **Burden-Shifting Framework**: Plaintiff must establish a prima facie case; employer must then produce a legitimate non-discriminatory reason; burden shifts back to plaintiff to demonstrate pretext.
* **Abrogation of Common Law**: Exclusively supersedes and abrogates common-law retaliatory discharge claims in Tennessee for refusal to participate in illegal acts.

## Statutory Text
(a) No employee shall be discharged or terminated solely for refusing to participate in, or for refusing to remain silent about, illegal activities.
(b) Any employee terminated in violation of subsection (a) shall have a cause of action against the employer for retaliatory discharge and any other damages to which the employee may be entitled...
(c) In any civil cause of action for retaliatory discharge brought pursuant to this section... the plaintiff shall have the burden of establishing a prima facie case of retaliatory discharge. If the plaintiff satisfies this burden, the burden shall then be on the defendant to produce evidence that one (1) or more legitimate, nondiscriminatory reasons existed for the plaintiff's discharge...
