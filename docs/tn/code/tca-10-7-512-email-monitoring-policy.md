# Tenn. Code Ann. § 10-7-512 — Electronic mail systems; monitoring policy required

## What this section requires (plain-English)

If a state agency, institution, or political subdivision **operates or maintains an email system**, it must adopt a **written policy** that covers:

- whether email communications are monitored;
- the circumstances under which monitoring occurs; and
- a statement that employee email correspondence **may be a public record** and **may be subject to public inspection** under Tennessee public records law.

## Operational use (county-ready)

- This is a **policy requirement**: agencies should have a written email monitoring policy on file.
- The policy must include a **public-records notice** (email can be a public record).

## Statutory text

(a) On or before July 1, 2000, the state or any agency, institution, or political subdivision thereof that operates or maintains an electronic mail communications system shall adopt a written policy on any monitoring of electronic mail communications and the circumstances under which it will be conducted.

(b) The policy shall include a statement that correspondence of the employee in the form of electronic mail may be a public record under the public records law and may be subject to public inspection under this part.

Acts 1999, ch. 304, § 2.

## Notes / cross-refs

- Pairs with TPRA’s core “public records” concepts in § 10-7-503.
- This section does not itself define what is or isn’t a public record; it requires a policy acknowledging that email **may** be.
