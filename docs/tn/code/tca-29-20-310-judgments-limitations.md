## Practitioner Summary
This statute establishes the operational mechanics of GTLA litigation. It prohibits suing individual employees when the county's immunity is removed, caps financial damages, and authorizes counties to indemnify their employees and volunteers.

## Key Practice Points
* **Employee Immunity**: A plaintiff cannot sue an individual county employee for negligence if the county's immunity is removed. The county is the sole proper defendant (unless the employee's act was willful, malicious, criminal, or involved health care liability).
* **Indemnification Authorization**: Counties possess the local option to insure or indemnify their employees and volunteers for claims, capped at the statutory limits of liability under § 29-20-403 (generally $300k per person, $700k per occurrence).
* **COVID-19 Protections**: Claims arising from COVID-19 require clear and convincing evidence of willful, malicious, or criminal acts.

## Statutory Text
(1) The court, before holding a governmental entity liable for damages, must first determine that the employee's or employees' act or acts were negligent and the proximate cause of plaintiff's injury, that the employee or employees acted within the scope of their employment and that none of the exceptions listed in § 29-20-205 are applicable to the facts before the court.
(b) No claim may be brought against an employee or judgment entered against an employee for damages for which the immunity of the governmental entity is removed by this chapter unless the claim is one for health care liability brought against a health care practitioner...
(c) No claim may be brought against an employee or judgment entered against an employee for injury proximately caused by an act or omission of the employee within the scope of the employee's employment for which the governmental entity is immune in any amount in excess of the amounts established for governmental entities in § 29-20-403, unless the act or omission was willful, malicious, criminal, or performed for personal financial gain...
