## Practitioner Summary
The definitive Tennessee Supreme Court ruling on police pursuit liability under the Governmental Tort Liability Act (GTLA). The Court reversed prior precedent, holding that an officer's decision to commence or continue a high-speed chase constitutes "conduct" that can be the proximate cause of injury to an innocent third party, exposing the county to liability.

## Key Practice Points
* **Definition of "Conduct"**: Negligent "conduct" under T.C.A. § 55-8-108(e) is not limited to the physical operation of the police vehicle (e.g., steering or braking); it explicitly includes the officer's discretionary decision to initiate or continue a high-speed chase.
* **Abrogation of "No Proximate Cause" Rule**: The Court rejected the per se rule that a fleeing suspect's negligence automatically supersedes police negligence. If an officer's unreasonable pursuit triggers the suspect's dangerous flight, the officer's conduct is a proximate cause.
* **The Balancing Test**: Whether a pursuit is reasonable is a jury question requiring the weighing of the risk of injury to innocent third parties against the law enforcement interest in apprehending the suspect (considering speed, weather, traffic, and the danger the suspect poses).

## Case Facts & Disposition
A Hamilton County Sheriff's deputy observed a suspect speeding and driving recklessly. When the deputy activated his lights and siren, the suspect fled. A high-speed chase ensued at speeds exceeding 100 mph. The suspect eventually lost control, crossed the center line, and collided head-on with a vehicle, killing three innocent teenagers.

The victims' families sued Hamilton County for negligence. The trial court and Court of Appeals granted summary judgment to the county, relying on prior cases (*Kennedy* and *Nevill*) which held that police conduct is not the proximate cause of a third-party collision. The Supreme Court reversed and remanded for trial, overruling those prior cases and establishing that an unreasonable decision to pursue a fleeing suspect exposes the county to liability under the GTLA.
