## Practitioner Summary
A landmark Tennessee Supreme Court ruling interpreting the "Discretionary Function Exception" of the GTLA (T.C.A. § 29-20-205). The Court formally adopted the "planning-operational test" to determine when a government entity is immune from tort lawsuits regarding its decision-making.

## Key Practice Points
* **Planning / Policy Decisions = Immune**: High-level decisions regarding the allocation of resources, budget constraints, or establishing broad county policies (e.g., deciding *not* to create a county-wide mowing program due to budget) are completely immune from suit.
* **Operational Decisions = Not Immune**: Day-to-day implementation of those policies (e.g., a county mower operator carelessly missing a patch of grass that obscures a stop sign) is operational and not shielded by immunity.
* **Tactical Application**: This is the primary defense used by county attorneys to get infrastructure design lawsuits dismissed before trial. 

## Case Facts & Disposition
A child was struck by a vehicle after exiting a school bus. The plaintiff sued the city, alleging the bus route and stop location were negligently designed. The city argued it was immune because establishing bus routes is a discretionary act. The Supreme Court established the planning-operational test. It ruled that the initial creation and design of the bus routing policy was a "planning" decision (immune), but the failure of the bus driver to follow existing safety protocols while executing the route was an "operational" failure (not immune).
