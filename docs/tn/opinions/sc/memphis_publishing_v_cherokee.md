## Practitioner Summary
The Tennessee Supreme Court established the "functional equivalency" test for the Tennessee Public Records Act (TPRA). The Court ruled that a private, non-profit corporation performing privatized governmental services operates as the functional equivalent of a state agency, meaning its records are fully subject to public inspection under the TPRA.

## Key Practice Points
* **Functional Equivalency Test**: Courts will look at the totality of circumstances to determine if a private entity's records are public. Key factors include: (1) the level of government funding, (2) the extent of government control/oversight, and (3) whether the entity performs a governmental function.
* **Closing the Privatization Loophole**: Government agencies cannot shield records from public scrutiny by contractually delegating public duties to private "independent contractors."
* **Audit Authority**: If an entity is deemed a functional equivalent of a government agency, the State Comptroller has the same access to its records as the public.

## Case Facts & Disposition
A newspaper sought records from Cherokee, a non-profit brokering state-subsidized child care. Cherokee refused, claiming it was a private independent contractor, not a state agency. The Supreme Court reversed the Court of Appeals, holding that because Cherokee performed a public function, was 99% government-funded, and was subject to state oversight, it was the functional equivalent of a state agency. Cherokee was ordered to open its records.
