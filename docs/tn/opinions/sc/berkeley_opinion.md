## Practitioner Summary
In a unanimous decision, the TN Supreme Court held that under the Uniform Arbitration Act (UAA), Tennessee courts **only** have subject matter jurisdiction to confirm an award if the underlying agreement specifically provided for arbitration to occur *within* Tennessee. 

## Key Practice Points
* **Jurisdictional Trigger**: Even if the parties are in TN, if the contract mandates arbitration in another state (e.g., PA), TN courts cannot confirm the award.
* **UAA Interpretation**: The Court strictly construed TCA § 29-5-323; "providing for arbitration in this state" is a prerequisite for subject matter jurisdiction.
* **Remote Arbitration**: Note that even though the arbitrator was "present" via Zoom in TN, the "seat" of arbitration defined in the contract (Philadelphia) was the controlling factor.

## Case Facts & Disposition
Berkeley (Consultants) sued Southern (Client) over unpaid fees. Arbitration happened via Zoom (Arbitrator in PA). Berkeley tried to confirm the award in Shelby County, TN.
**Disposition**: Dismissed for lack of subject matter jurisdiction.
