## Practitioner Summary
A landmark Tennessee Supreme Court decision establishing a constitutionally-based attorney-client exception to the Tennessee Open Meetings Act (TOMA). The Court recognized that applying the open meetings mandate to strategy sessions regarding pending litigation violates the separation of powers and an attorney's ethical duties.

## Key Practice Points
* **The Attorney-Client Exception**: A public governing body may meet privately behind closed doors with its legal counsel solely to discuss pending, present, or impending litigation.
* **Limitation on Authority**: The exception is strictly limited to receiving legal advice and discussing litigation strategy. Once the public body begins deliberating or deciding what action to take (e.g., voting on a settlement), the meeting must be open to the public.
* **Agent Presence**: The presence of the board's designated agents (e.g., a chief negotiator) during the attorney-client meeting does not waive the privilege.

## Case Facts & Disposition
During a labor dispute between the Smith County Education Association and the Board of Education, the Board met privately with its attorney and its chief negotiator to discuss the ongoing litigation regarding insurance premiums and union dues. No public notice of these meetings was given.

The plaintiffs sued, alleging a violation of the Tennessee Open Meetings Act (T.C.A. § 8-44-102(a)), which states all meetings of a governing body must be open "except as provided by the Tennessee Constitution." The Court of Appeals found a TOMA violation. The Supreme Court of Tennessee reversed, fashioning the attorney-client exception. The Court reasoned that forcing an attorney to disclose litigation strategy in a public forum violates the Supreme Court's exclusive authority to regulate the ethical duties of attorneys under Article II of the Tennessee Constitution.
