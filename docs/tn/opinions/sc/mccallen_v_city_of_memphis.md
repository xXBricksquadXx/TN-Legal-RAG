## Practitioner Summary
The Tennessee Supreme Court defined the legal standard for reviewing municipal and county zoning decisions, distinguishing between legislative and administrative acts. The Court held that a city council's approval of a planned development under existing zoning guidelines is an administrative act subject to review via common law writ of certiorari, requiring high deference to the local governmental body.

## Key Practice Points
* **Administrative vs. Legislative Acts**: Enacting or amending a general zoning ordinance is legislative (reviewed by declaratory judgment); applying existing zoning guidelines to a specific project (like a planned development permit) is administrative (reviewed by common law certiorari).
* **High Judicial Deference**: Courts must not substitute their judgment for the broad discretionary authority of the local governmental body.
* **Standard of Review**: Administrative land use decisions will only be invalidated if the decision is clearly illegal, arbitrary, or capricious. If "any possible reason" justifies the action, it must be upheld.

## Case Facts & Disposition
Property owners sued the City of Memphis to invalidate a city council resolution approving a commercial planned development (a convenience store and retail building) in an area predominantly zoned for multiple-dwelling residential use. The Land Use Control Board approved the plan despite staff opposition, and the city council subsequently voted 7-4 to approve the development. 

The trial court invalidated the resolution, citing a lack of substantial evidence, and the Court of Appeals affirmed. The Supreme Court reversed both lower courts, ruling that the council's action was administrative and that the lower courts failed to give proper deference. Because there was a rational basis and evidence supporting the plan's compatibility, the council’s decision was not arbitrary or capricious and was therefore valid.
