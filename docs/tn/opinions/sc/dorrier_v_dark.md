## Practitioner Summary
In this landmark 1976 decision, the Tennessee Supreme Court upheld the constitutionality of the Tennessee Open Meetings Act (TOMA). The Court firmly rejected arguments that the Act was void for vagueness, abridged free speech, or was arbitrary for failing to allow closed "executive sessions" for sensitive topics.

## Key Practice Points
* **No Executive Sessions**: The Court affirmed that TOMA provides **no exceptions** for closed meetings (e.g., for personnel, disciplinary hearings, or pending litigation). If a governing body is deliberating toward a decision, it must be open.
* **Definition of "Governing Body"**: The Act applies broadly to any board, commission, or authority whose origin and authority trace back to legislative action and whose members make decisions on public policy or administration.
* **Nullification**: The statutory sanction for violating TOMA is the absolute nullification of the action taken during the illegal meeting.

## Case Facts & Disposition
A tenured teacher (Dark) was terminated following a hearing by the Metropolitan Board of Education. Dark argued the termination was void because the Board deliberated and formulated its decision in a closed executive session. The Chancery Court agreed, voiding the Board's action. The Supreme Court affirmed the lower court, cementing the Sunshine Law's strict "no secret meetings" policy.
