## Practitioner Summary
This case establishes that a government entity cannot require a citizen to appear in person to request or inspect public records. The Court ruled that imposing such unlawful conditions constitutes a "willful" denial of access under the TPRA, thereby justifying an award of attorney's fees to the requester.

## Key Practice Points
* **No In-Person Requirement**: Public records requests cannot be conditioned on the requester making a physical, in-person appearance at the government office.
* **Willful Violation Trigger**: Denying access based on an improper interpretation of the law (such as inventing an in-person requirement) is a willful violation.
* **Attorney's Fees**: A willful violation of the TPRA opens the government entity to liability for the requester's attorney's fees and costs.

## Case Facts & Disposition
Alex Friedmann, managing editor of Prison Legal News, requested public records (contracts/policies) from the Marshall County Jail. The Sheriff refused to process the request unless Friedmann appeared in person. The Appellate Court held that the county's refusal was a willful violation of the TPRA and ruled that an award of attorney fees and costs was appropriate.
