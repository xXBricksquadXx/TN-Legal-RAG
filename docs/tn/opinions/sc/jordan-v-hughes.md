## Practitioner Summary
The Tennessee Court of Appeals clarified the legal requirements for establishing an implied dedication and public acceptance of a private road into the official county highway system under Tennessee common law and the County Uniform Highway Law (CUHL). The Court affirmed that public use alone is insufficient to impose maintenance duties or establish a public county road without clear, unequivocal evidence of the landowner's intent to dedicate and affirmative public/governmental acceptance.

## Key Practice Points
* **Elements of Implied Dedication**: To establish an implied dedication of a roadway, there must be a showing of: (1) the landowner’s offer/intent to dedicate the land to public use, inferred from overt acts or acquiescence, and (2) acceptance by the public or governmental authority.
* **Burden of Proof**: The party asserting that a road is public bears the heavy burden of showing intent and acceptance by clear and convincing evidence.
* **Public Maintenance vs. Permissive Use**: Mere casual maintenance by county highway equipment or general permissive public travel does not automatically transform a private easement or driveway into an official public county road under T.C.A. Title 54.

## Case Facts & Disposition
Property owners (Jordan) brought an action against neighboring landowners (Hughes) and the local county government seeking a judicial declaration that an unpaved access road running across private property had become a public county road by implied dedication and long-standing public use, thereby requiring county maintenance. 

The trial court ruled that the road remained private, finding insufficient evidence of an intent to dedicate or formal acceptance by the county highway department. The Tennessee Court of Appeals affirmed, holding that occasional public passage and incidental county work did not meet the strict evidentiary threshold required for implied dedication or public road creation under Tennessee law.
