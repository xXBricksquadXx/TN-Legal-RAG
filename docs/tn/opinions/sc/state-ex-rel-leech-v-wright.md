## Practitioner Summary
The Tennessee Supreme Court established the definitive procedural and evidentiary standards under the Tennessee Ouster Act (T.C.A. § 8-47-101 et seq.). The Court held that an ouster proceeding is a "speedy summary proceeding" requiring clear and convincing evidence of knowing, willful misconduct or neglect, emphasizing that administrative errors or poor judgment do not satisfy the statutory threshold for removing an official from public office.

## Key Practice Points
* **Clear and Convincing Evidentiary Standard**: Removal under T.C.A. § 8-47-101 requires proof by clear and convincing evidence that an official "knowingly or willfully" committed misconduct or failed to perform mandatory duties.
* **Distinction Between Negligence and Misconduct**: Mere mistakes, errors in judgment, or administrative incompetence—unaccompanied by intentional or bad-faith intent—do not justify forfeiture of public office.
* **Speedy Summary Proceeding**: Ouster actions take statutory precedence over other civil and criminal matters on the court docket, allowing only a petition and answer as valid pleadings (T.C.A. § 8-47-114).

## Statutory Text / Holding
The State of Tennessee, on the relation of the Attorney General, filed an ouster action against a public official alleging knowing and willful misconduct in office involving municipal fund allocations and administrative duties. The Tennessee Supreme Court affirmed the principles governing the Ouster Act, cautioning lower courts that "shreds of human imperfections gathered together to mold charges of official dereliction should be carefully scanned before a reputable officer is removed from office." The Court established that to strip a democratically elected or appointed officer of their position, the record must demonstrate clear, convincing, and intentional disregard of official duty.
