## Practitioner Summary
This foundational Court of Appeals case establishes that public bodies cannot ratify decisions made in violation of the Open Meetings Act through perfunctory, "rubber-stamp" voting. It also clarifies that out-of-state "retreats" where public business is discussed constitute illegal meetings if not properly noticed.

## Key Practice Points
* **"Meeting" & "Deliberation" Defined**: A retreat where members examine, consult, or weigh arguments for/against a proposed action constitutes a "meeting" under TOMA, even if no formal vote is taken.
* **Curing a Violation**: A governing body cannot cure a Sunshine Law violation by holding a subsequent meeting simply to rubber-stamp the prior invalid action. Curing requires a **new and substantial public reconsideration** of the issues.
* **Adequate Notice**: Providing misleading or inadequate public notice for a gathering violates TOMA, rendering any actions taken void.

## Case Facts & Disposition
A school board held a "retreat" in Kentucky to discuss school "clustering" without providing adequate public notice. The court ruled this was a TOMA violation. Furthermore, the court held that a subsequent public board meeting attempting to quickly vote and ratify the decisions made at the illegal retreat was insufficient to cure the original violation.
