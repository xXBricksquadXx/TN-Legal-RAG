## Practitioner Summary
The TN Supreme Court held that the warrantless use of high-resolution drone surveillance to peer into the "curtilage" of a private residence violates Article I, Section 7 of the Tennessee Constitution. While "open fields" are not protected, the intimate area immediately surrounding the home (the curtilage) maintains a reasonable expectation of privacy that aerial technology cannot bypass without a warrant.

## Key Practice Points
* **Curtilage Protection**: Reaffirms that the curtilage is an extension of the home for constitutional purposes.
* **Technology vs. Constitution**: Explicitly limits the "Open Fields" doctrine when advanced surveillance (drones) is used to target private residential spaces.
* **Warrant Requirement**: Law enforcement must secure a warrant before deploying drones for residential surveillance.

## Case Facts & Disposition
State used drones to photograph a fenced backyard for drug activity without a warrant.
**Disposition**: Evidence suppressed; lower court reversed.
